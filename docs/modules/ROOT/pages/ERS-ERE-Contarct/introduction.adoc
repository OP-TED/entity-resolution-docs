== Introduction

The Entity Resolution Engine (ERE) is a service that provides entity resolution capabilities. Together with the Entity Resolution Service (ERS), it forms the Entity Resolution System (ERSys), whose purpose is to enable human users and external systems to resolve entities within their data in a consistent and interoperable manner. +
Within this architecture, the ERE is responsible for _clustering logic and canonical identity materialisation_, while the ERS is responsible for _controlled exposure, time-budget discipline, and persistence of the latest recorded outcomes_. Their cooperation is governed exclusively through the technical contract defined in this document. +
The ERE accepts structured entity mentions as input, including descriptions of organisations, persons, publications, or other domain entities. These descriptions are provided as text-based serialisations, for example RDF/XML or RDF/Turtle, with the concrete format indicated through a MIME type. Based on this input, the ERE performs clustering according to its internal resolution logic and produces clustering outcomes consisting of a cluster identifier and associated assessment signals such as similarity and confidence scores. The cluster identifier generated by the ERE constitutes the _canonical identifier_ exposed by ERS. +
Although initially developed within the TED-SWS context, the ERE is designed as an independent and replaceable component. Any implementation that conforms to the normative contract defined herein may interoperate with ERS. This contract establishes a stable and implementation-independent boundary between ERS and ERE. It defines the message structures, correlation semantics, idempotent processing expectations, and canonical identifier derivation rules, without prescribing internal algorithms, data structures, or optimisation strategies. +
Multiple ERE implementations may exist, ranging from baseline to advanced variants. All such implementations shall conform to this contract. By stabilising the boundary semantics, ERSys preserves _engine replaceability_, architectural clarity, and long-term evolvability.

=== Canonical Identifier Derivation and Stability (Normative)

Stable and reproducible identifier semantics are essential for asynchronous processing, idempotent request handling, and consistent exposure of clustering outcomes. This section defines the normative rules governing canonical identifier value derivation and stability within ERSys. +
Within ERSys, a single identifier value space is used. The _cluster identifier generated by the ERE constitutes the canonical identifier_ exposed by ERS. There is no separate or elevated identifier layer. The same identifier value is used consistently for clustering, exposure, replay, and downstream reference. +
Canonical identifier values shall be deterministically derived from the components of the entity mention identifier, namely: +(source_id, request_id, entity_type)+. +
The normative derivation rule is: +
+SHA256(concat(source_id, request_id, entity_type))+ +
All conformant ERS and ERE implementations shall implement this derivation rule identically. No component shall introduce alternative derivation strategies, centralised allocation mechanisms, or non-deterministic identifier generation. +
Deterministic derivation ensures that:

* repeated or replayed resolution requests produce stable identifier values;
* provisional singleton clusters generated under time-budget constraints remain stable if later confirmed;
* clustering outcomes can be correlated across asynchronous exchanges without additional coordination.
Identifier value stability is distinct from cluster membership stability. While the identifier value of a cluster remains stable once generated, the set of entity mentions assigned to that cluster may evolve over time as new evidence is processed or re-resolution occurs. Conversely, an entity mention may be reassigned to a different cluster as clustering outcomes change. Stability therefore applies to identifier values, not to membership immutability. +
The purpose of this approach is to eliminate centralised identifier governance and prevent tight coupling between ERS and ERE. By relying on a shared deterministic derivation rule, ERSys enables independent evolution of ERE implementations while preserving interoperability, idempotency, and long-term referential stability. +
This section is the sole normative specification of canonical identifier value derivation within ERSys. All other sections and artefacts shall reference this rule rather than restating or redefining it.

=== Contract scope and realisation model

image::ROOT:L2 - Contract Realisation and Messaging Mediation (ERS-ERE).png[]

This diagram illustrates the structural scope of the ERS–ERE technical contract and clarifies the responsibilities of the participating components. +
The ERS–ERE contract defines an asynchronous application interface governing the exchange of resolution requests and clustering outcomes between ERS and ERE. It constitutes the sole normative agreement between the two components and defines their cooperation independently of any specific implementation. +
Both ERS and ERE realise this contract, but with distinct responsibilities. The ERE holds _clustering authority_: it evaluates entity mentions, executes its internal resolution logic, and produces clustering outcomes, including the cluster identifier that constitutes the canonical identifier. The ERS holds _exposure and integration authority_: it forwards resolution and re-resolution requests, enforces client-facing time budgets, records the latest clustering outcome per mention, and exposes canonical identifiers through its public interfaces. +
The contract is intentionally decoupled from the messaging middleware used for transport. While ERS and ERE exchange messages via a broker or messaging infrastructure, the choice of technology, delivery guarantees, and deployment topology are outside the scope of this contract. The contract specifies what messages are exchanged and under which semantic rules, not how they are transported. +
This separation ensures that the contract remains stable even if the messaging infrastructure evolves, that ERS and ERE implementations remain independently replaceable, and that clustering authority, exposure responsibility, and transport concerns are kept clearly distinct. +
The detailed interaction semantics governed by this contract are described in the following sections.

=== Interaction model and idempotency

The ERE exposes its functionality through an asynchronous publish/subscribe interaction model. The ERS publishes resolution and re-resolution requests to a requests channel, and the ERE subscribes to this channel to receive and process them. The ERE, in turn, publishes clustering outcomes to a responses channel, which the ERS subscribes to in order to receive and integrate the results.

The primary interaction governed by this contract is the entity resolution request. In response, the ERE produces a _clustering outcome_ consisting of a primary cluster assignment for the entity mention and associated assessment signals, such as similarity and confidence scores. Where applicable, alternative cluster identifiers may also be included to support transparency and downstream reasoning. +
This interaction model is intentionally asynchronous. Submitting a request does not yield an immediate response; instead, clustering outcomes are published at a later time once computation completes. This reflects the computational variability of entity resolution and allows ERS to enforce client-facing time budgets without blocking on engine execution. +
Because communication is asynchronous and delivery guarantees may be at-least-once, identical requests or responses may be observed multiple times. All conformant implementations shall therefore implement _idempotent processing semantics_. Repeated submissions of the same request shall not create duplicate state, and repeated delivery of the same clustering outcome shall not result in inconsistent assignments. +
Clustering authority resides in the ERE. The cluster identifier produced in a clustering outcome constitutes the canonical identifier. Upon receipt of a valid outcome, the ERS correlates it using the entity mention identifier triad and records the _latest received clustering outcome_ for that mention. Integration discipline is applied at the ERS boundary, but canonical identity is determined by the ERE. +
Outcomes may arrive without a preceding request in cases of engine-initiated reclustering. Such outcomes are processed under the same correlation and idempotency rules. +
The interaction model defined here specifies the semantic exchange between ERS and ERE. It does not mandate a particular messaging middleware or transport technology. Any infrastructure may be used, provided that message ordering, duplication, and delivery characteristics are handled in a manner consistent with the idempotent semantics defined in this contract. +
