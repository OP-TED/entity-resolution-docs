== Purpose, scope, and conceptual framing of this glossary

This glossary defines the authoritative meaning of terms used throughout the Entity Resolution System (ERSys) architecture, use cases, contracts, and decision records. Its purpose is to stabilise interpretation, prevent semantic drift, and ensure that all stakeholders reason about identity, resolution, and governance using a shared and precise vocabulary. +
The glossary is normative. Terms defined here constrain the interpretation of architectural views, use cases, APIs, and implementation artefacts. Where terminology commonly used in data integration, master data management, or knowledge graph practice is reused with a more specific or narrower meaning, the definitions in this glossary take precedence over any colloquial or external interpretation. +
The glossary distinguishes rigorously between:

* *Real-world entities*, which exist independently of the system,
* *Information artefacts*, which describe or refer to those entities,
* *Identity constructs*, which ERSys exposes and whose semantics are determined by the ERE,
* *System roles and components*, which perform actions and hold responsibility.
These distinctions are foundational. Many commonly used terms such as _entity_, _record_, _identifier_, or _cluster_ are overloaded in practice. In ERSys, each term has a precise and constrained meaning that must be respected consistently across all documentation and implementations.

=== Conceptual framing

ERSys is founded on a strict conceptual separation between real-world entities, information artefacts, and system-managed identifiers. +
Real-world entities are the referents that exist independently of the system: persons, organisations, objects, or abstract domain concepts. They are not stored, versioned, or directly manipulated by ERSys. The system does not claim to possess or model real-world entities themselves. It operates only on information artefacts that refer to them. +
ERSys processes entity mentions, which are externally supplied records describing or referring to real-world entities. Each mention carries a representation in a given serialised form. Representations convey descriptive content only. They may be incomplete, duplicated, inconsistent, or evolve over time. Descriptive content never constitutes identity. +
Identity in ERSys is realised through clusters managed by the Entity Resolution Engine (ERE). A cluster groups entity mentions that the engine determines to be co-referent under its resolution logic. The identifier assigned to a cluster (clusterId) is the canonical identifier exposed by ERS. +
Canonical identity in ERSys is therefore equivalent to cluster identity in the ERE. The ERE cluster store is the single source of truth for identity semantics. ERS does not independently govern or redefine identity; it projects and exposes the latest clustering outcome returned by the ERE. +
Canonical identifiers are stable and non-reused values that function as proxy references for downstream systems. Stability means that identifier values are not reassigned to unrelated clusters. It does not imply immutability of mention-to-cluster assignment. A mention’s canonical identifier may change over time as the ERE returns updated clustering outcomes. +
To reconcile asynchronous engine computation with bounded client interaction, ERS may issue a provisional singleton cluster identifier when the engine does not respond within the configured execution window. Such provisional identifiers are valid canonical identifiers until the ERE confirms or replaces them. Subsequent engine outcomes may therefore update the canonical assignment without retroactively altering prior API responses. +
Clusters are not descriptive objects. They are identity constructs. Any representation associated with a cluster is treated as data and does not redefine identity semantics. Descriptive artefacts may change without affecting the canonical identifier. +
User actions and corrective operations are expressed as recommendations to the ERE. They do not override or freeze identity. The authoritative clustering outcome always remains the responsibility of the engine. +
This conceptual framing establishes ERSys as:

* *identity-projection-centric rather than governance-centric*,
* *cluster-authoritative rather than registry-authoritative*,
* *asynchronous in computation but bounded in exposure*, and
* strictly separated between descriptive content and identity constructs.
All glossary definitions that follow must be interpreted under this engine-authoritative identity model.

=== Structure of the glossary

The glossary is divided into two sections:

* *Information-oriented concepts*, which define domain concepts, identity constructs, and information artefacts independent of runtime behaviour or system decomposition.
* *System-oriented concepts*, which define actors, components, operations, and interaction patterns that realise those concepts at runtime.
Unless explicitly stated otherwise, glossary terms are used consistently and normatively across all ERSys documentation. Any deviation from a glossary definition must be explicitly stated and is limited to the stated context. +
The glossary does not define implementation details, algorithms, persistence models, or user-interface behaviour. It defines _meaning_, _authority_, and _scope of responsibility_. Any change to the meaning of a glossary term constitutes a semantic change to the system and must be reflected explicitly in the architectural baseline and, where relevant, in the Architecture Decision Records.
This discipline ensures that discussions about entity resolution in ERSys remain precise: when we say entity, canonical entity, cluster, identifier, or decision, we always mean the same thing, and we can reason about evolution, governance, and correctness without hidden assumptions.
