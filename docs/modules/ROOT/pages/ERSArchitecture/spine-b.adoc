=== 8.3 Spine B: Asynchronous engine interaction and outcome integration (ERS–ERE)

*Intent.* Spine B defines the asynchronous interaction between ERS and the Entity Resolution Engine (ERE) for resolution requests and clustering outcomes. This spine is the architectural shock absorber of ERSys: it makes explicit that messaging is at-least-once, outcomes can be late or duplicated, and the engine may publish outcomes even when ERS did not just send a request (for example, due to engine-initiated reclustering). The ERE is authoritative for clustering outcomes, and ERS is responsible for correlating, recording, and exposing the latest outcome per mention.

*Narrative aligned to the simplified diagram*

*Publish a resolution request (asynchronous).* ERS may publish a resolution request to the messaging middleware. This is typically triggered by Spine A (intake) or Spine D (curation recommendation), and it always carries the correlation triad _(source_id, request_id, entity_type)_ plus the Entity Mention. Optionally, ERS may include the optional ERS→ERE fields defined in the xref:ERS-ERE-Contract/interface.adoc[ERS–ERE contract]:

* `excluded_cluster_ids` (negative evidence), and/or
* `proposed_cluster_ids` (preferred placement), including provisional singleton placement.

These fields are not governance overrides; they are inputs to engine evaluation.

*Deliver to the engine under at-least-once semantics.* The messaging middleware delivers the request to the ERE asynchronously. Duplicates can occur, and delivery ordering is not guaranteed.

*Produce and publish an authoritative outcome.* The ERE performs its internal resolution logic and publishes a resolution result that includes:

* the correlation triad _(source_id, request_id, entity_type)_,
* the primary *`cluster_id` (canonical identifier)*,
* top alternative `cluster_id` values,
* for each cluster, the related scoring metadata: `confidence_score` and `similarity_score`.

Outcomes may be *solicited* (in response to an ERS request) or *unsolicited* (for example, engine-initiated reclustering). Both are handled using the same integration pattern.

*Validate, correlate, and record.* ERS validates the message envelope and schema against the ERS–ERE Technical Contract.

* If the delivery violates the contract (for example, missing triad, invalid schema), ERS records the violation for traceability and ignores the payload for decision state.
* If the delivery is acceptable (including duplicates or late arrivals), ERS correlates by the triad and updates its *latest decision record per mention*. This includes recording the current `cluster_id`, top alternatives (as provided), `context_id` for correlation, and timestamps.

*Expose latest-assignment-wins.* Completed client responses are not retroactively rewritten. However, subsequent lookups and refreshBulk delta exposure reflect the latest authoritative assignment recorded by ERS: the HTTP response is fixed, but the canonical identity exposed by the system may evolve as the engine publishes improved outcomes.

*Ordering and deduplication rule (normative).* ERS shall treat ERE outcomes as _idempotent but not ordered_. For a given triad, ERS shall accept an outcome only if it is not stale with respect to the currently recorded decision. Staleness shall be determined using a contract-defined monotonic outcome marker (for example, `engine_outcome_timestamp` or `engine_outcome_sequence`) produced by the engine. If the marker indicates the outcome is older than the stored one, ERS shall ignore it for decision state (but may record it for operational traceability).

*Key behavioural commitments shown*

* ERS–ERE communication is *exclusively asynchronous* and governed by the ERS–ERE Technical Contract.
* ERE outcomes are *authoritative clustering outcomes*, not advisory proposals.
* Correlation and idempotent absorption are performed using the triad _(source_id, request_id, entity_type)_, and the integration is tolerant to duplicates and late arrivals.
* Optional `excluded_cluster_ids` and `proposed_cluster_ids` are treated as input evidence. The ERE remains binding for the resulting cluster assignment.
* ERS records and exposes only what it needs: the *latest decision record per mention* (current `cluster_id`, alternatives, `context_id`, timestamps). There is no Canonical Entity Registry or canonical lineage store inside ERS.

*Authoritative state touched*

* *Decision Store* (latest decision per mention): updated on acceptable outcome deliveries (including late or duplicate deliveries) to reflect the latest recorded assignment.
* *Contract violation log / operational trace*: updated when invalid deliveries are received (for investigation and robustness), without mutating decision state.
* *Request Registry*: not mutated by Spine B; used implicitly for correlation and idempotency handling.
