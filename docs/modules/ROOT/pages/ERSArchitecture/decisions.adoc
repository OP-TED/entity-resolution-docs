= Architectural Decision Baseline

This section defines the normative architectural baseline of the ERSys. It states the binding architectural principles and invariants that constrain all implementations and govern system behaviour independently of technology choices, deployment models, or internal algorithms. All these principles are reflected and further specified in xref:AnnexeC-ADRs/index.adoc[Annexe C]. Any change to these principles requires corresponding revision of the relevant ADRs.

All use cases, architectural views, interaction flows, contracts, and decision records in this architecture shall be interpreted as applications of the baseline defined in this section.

== Architectural principles

The following architectural principles are binding. They define structural invariants of ERSys and constrain all design and implementation decisions.

=== Engine-Authoritative Identity

Canonical identity is determined exclusively by clustering outcomes produced by the Entity Resolution Engine (ERE). The `cluster_id` returned by the ERE is the canonical identifier. No component, actor, or external system may assert, alter, invalidate, or finalise canonical identity outside the ERE contract and the provisional assignment mechanism defined by ERS.

ERS records and exposes the latest resolution decision per mention. It does not introduce an independent identity governance layer.

=== Bounded Exposure with Provisional Continuity

Client-facing interactions are bounded by explicit time budgets enforced by ERS. If the ERE responds within the execution window, its `cluster_id` is returned. If it does not, ERS issues a deterministically derived singleton `cluster_id` in accordance with the canonical identifier derivation rule defined in the ERS–ERE Technical Contract. Deterministic derivation applies to provisional singleton placements only. ERS forwards this placement to the ERE for authoritative evaluation.

Identity continuity at the API boundary is guaranteed without transferring clustering authority away from the ERE.

=== Non-Reuse and Stability of Canonical Identifiers

Canonical identifiers are stable and shall not be reused for unrelated clusters. Stability guarantees non-reuse of identifier values. It does not guarantee immutability of mention-to-cluster assignment. A mention may be reassigned when the ERE produces a new authoritative outcome.

=== Latest-Decision Persistence Only

ERS persists only the latest resolution decision per mention in the Decision Store, including the current `cluster_id` and the top N alternatives returned by the most recent ERE outcome. Replacement of a cluster assignment is atomic at the level of the Decision Store and does not create parallel authoritative states. ERS does not maintain canonical lineage, historical cluster graphs, or an independent canonical entity registry. Persistence records authoritative engine outcomes and does not derive additional identity semantics.

A separate User Action Log records human recommendations as a read-only trace. These records do not define or override canonical identity.

=== Recommendations Do Not Confer Identity

User actions and provisional ERS placements are transmitted to the ERE as recommendations. They influence evaluation but do not directly modify canonical identity. The authoritative assignment is always and exclusively the result of ERE clustering.

== Normative architectural invariants

The following invariants define the binding architectural baseline of ERSys. They apply across all components and conformant implementations and must hold independently of deployment topology or engine implementation details.

=== Canonical identifier stability within a deterministic value space

Canonical identifiers correspond to `cluster_id` values generated and managed by the ERE within a deterministic identifier value space. Identifier stability means that identifier values are generated in a predictable and non-arbitrary manner and are not reassigned to unrelated identity constructs. Stability does not imply immutability of mention-to-cluster assignment. A mention may be moved to another cluster when the ERE produces a new authoritative clustering outcome. Canonical status itself is defined by ERE clustering outcomes.

=== Single current decision per mention

For each entity mention, ERS shall maintain exactly one current authoritative resolution decision representing the latest cluster assignment returned by the ERE. Superseded assignments are replaced in the Decision Store. Historical canonical lineage is not maintained as part of authoritative identity state. Only the cluster assignment contained in the latest accepted engine outcome defines canonical identity at any given time.

=== Guaranteed identifier return within configured time budget

Client-facing resolution interactions are subject to explicit, configured time budgets enforced by ERS. For every valid resolution request, a canonical identifier shall be returned within the client-facing time budget. The identifier shall be either:

* the `cluster_id` produced by the ERE within the execution window, or
* a provisional singleton `cluster_id` issued by ERS when the execution window expires.

Subsequent authoritative outcomes produced by the ERE may replace a provisional assignment. The canonical identity exposed by ERS always reflects the latest authoritative clustering outcome recorded in the Decision Store and may change over time as new authoritative outcomes are received.

=== Engine authority over clustering state

The ERE is the sole authority for clustering logic and canonical identity determination. ERS records authoritative clustering outcomes returned by the ERE and exposes the resulting assignment state. ERS does not derive, reinterpret, or augment identity semantics beyond recording engine outcomes.

Intermediate or engine-internal state remains outside the identity contract and is not exposed as canonical identity.

=== ERS records are projections of authoritative ERE outcomes

The Decision Store contains only the latest authoritative cluster assignment per mention and the top N alternatives returned by the most recent ERE response.

The User Action Log records human recommendations as a read-only trace for accountability and potential training purposes. These records do not define canonical identity and do not override engine authority.

=== Delta consistency for assignment changes

Any change in cluster assignment for a mention shall update the state used for delta computation in bulk refresh operations. `refreshBulk` semantics depend exclusively on changes in recorded cluster assignments. No other state transition shall generate identity deltas.

=== Engine-controlled reclustering

Full cluster rebuild operations are responsibilities of the ERE. When the ERE performs reclustering and produces updated clustering outcomes, ERS shall record the latest authoritative assignments and apply delta tracking accordingly. ERS does not independently initiate or govern clustering recomputation logic.

== Interpretation and change control

The architectural principles and invariants defined in this section constitute the current normative baseline for ERSys.

The detailed Architecture Decision Records are maintained in xref:AnnexeC-ADRs/index.adoc[Annexe C]. Together with this section, they define the binding architectural rules governing identity semantics, authority boundaries, interaction contracts, state management, and cross-cutting constraints.

Any change that weakens, bypasses, or contradicts these principles, invariants, or ADRs constitutes an architectural change and requires explicit revision of this architecture and of the corresponding ADRs. Such changes shall not be introduced implicitly through implementation decisions, configuration choices, undocumented operational practices, or contract-level reinterpretation.
