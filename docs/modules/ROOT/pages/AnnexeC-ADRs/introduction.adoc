== Introduction and Index

This annex contains the Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) for the ERSys. An ADR is a decision artefact that captures a non-trivial architectural choice in a durable form. Its purpose is to ensure that intentional design decisions remain visible over time, especially when original context fades and behaviour risks being misinterpreted as accidental. +
The ADRs define the binding architectural invariants of ERSys. They are not a feature catalogue and not a detailed design specification. Instead, they constrain how the system may be structured and evolved, particularly in areas involving asynchronous interaction, replaceable engines, long-lived identifiers, and distributed responsibility. +
Taken together, the ADRs stabilise ERSys around a coherent model of identity, authority, interaction, and state separation. They provide a reference framework against which contracts, use cases, and implementation decisions can be evaluated.

=== Conceptual Frame Assumed by the ADRs

All ADRs in this annex share a common conceptual framing. ERSys does not manage real-world entities directly. It governs identity through system-level constructs such as entity mentions, cluster assignments, and canonical identifiers. Canonical identity is equivalent to cluster membership in ERE. +
Representations are descriptive artefacts. They do not define identity. Scores produced by engines are computational outputs. They do not constitute authoritative truth. Presentation-layer data is operational support and does not influence canonical identity. +
Across the ADR set:

* Authority is separated from computation.
* Canonical identity is defined by clustering, not by representation.
* ERS projects and exposes identity; ERE determines clustering.
* Stability is achieved by deterministic identifier rules and non-reuse, not by preventing assignment change.

This framing prevents semantic drift, avoids hidden governance layers, and ensures that identity, authority, and operational concerns remain clearly separated.

=== How the ADR set is systematised

The ADR set is organised as a compact catalogue of non-overlapping architectural invariants. Each ADR defines one binding rule that constrains contracts, diagrams, and implementation. The structure follows thematic series by concern rather than chronology.

* A-Series - Identity: *Defines canonical identity semantics: clusterId is the canonical identifier, singleton IDs are deterministic, identifiers are never reused, and assignment may change.
* B-Series - Authority: *Defines responsibility boundaries: ERE is authoritative for clustering; ERS is façade and projection layer; user actions are recommendations only. +
* C-Series - Interaction: *Defines client and messaging behaviour: synchronous client contract, asynchronous ERS–ERE exchange, provisional identifiers, idempotent integration. +
* D-Series - State & Synchronisation: *Defines authoritative stores and projection rules: ERE is source of truth; ERS stores latest placement only; refreshBulk exposes assignment deltas. +
* E-Series - Corrective Processing: *Defines re-resolution and reclustering semantics and their uniform integration. +
* F-Series - Representation Boundaries: *Separates identity from payload, preview, and pruning concerns. +
* G-Series - Cross-Cutting: *Constrain observability and security without altering identity or authority semantics. +

The organising principle is strict separation of identity, authority, interaction, state, and representation, ensuring a coherent engine-authoritative model without hidden governance layers.
