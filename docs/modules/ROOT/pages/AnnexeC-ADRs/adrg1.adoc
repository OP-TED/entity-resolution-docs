[#adr-g1n]
= ADR-G1N — Observability and Telemetry

[NOTE]
====
Status: Accepted
====

== Context and Problem Statement

ERS operates in a distributed, asynchronous environment where:

* Resolution requests are forwarded to ERE.
* Provisional identifiers may be issued.
* Cluster assignments may later change.
* Delta updates are exposed via refreshBulk.

Operational insight is required to understand request flow, timing behaviour, reassignment events, and integration outcomes.

However, observability must not:

* Influence clustering semantics.
* Delay or block resolution flows.
* Introduce a secondary source of authority over identity state.

A clear separation is therefore required between functional behaviour and telemetry behaviour.

== Decision

. *Best-Effort, Non-Blocking Telemetry*
.. Telemetry emission is best-effort and non-blocking.
.. Failure or delay in telemetry infrastructure shall not affect resolution processing, provisional issuance, projection updates, or delta exposure.
. *Metadata-Only Semantics*
.. Telemetry records contain metadata only.
.. They may include (possible metadata):
... mention identifier tuple (source_id, request_id, entity_type)
... cluster_id (where applicable)
... action_type
... Timestamps
... execution outcomes
... internal correlation identifiers
.. Full payload capture is not implied by default.
. *Authoritative State Is Not Derived from Telemetry*
.. Telemetry does not constitute authoritative clustering state.
.. Canonical identity and assignment are determined exclusively by ERE outcomes integrated into ERS projection stores.
.. Telemetry logs must not be used as a source of truth for cluster state.
. *Coverage of Identity Lifecycle Events*
.. Telemetry should capture externally observable identity events, including:
... Initial resolution
... Provisional identifier issuance
... Replacement of provisional identifiers
... User-triggered re-resolution
... Reclustering-driven reassignment
... refreshBulk delta exposure
.. These events are recorded for traceability, not for governance.
. *No Implicit Compliance Guarantees*
.. Telemetry constitutes an operational trace.
.. It does not imply regulatory-grade auditing, completeness guarantees, durability guarantees, or non-repudiation semantics.

== Scope and Applicability

This decision applies to telemetry emitted by ERS and its integration points with ERE.

It governs trace semantics, correlation rules, and the relationship between observability and functional behaviour.

Data retention policy, regulatory audit compliance, and security monitoring are out of scope.

== Considered Options

* *Blocking Telemetry Model.* Make observability infrastructure mandatory for request processing, failing functional operations if telemetry fails.
* *Full Payload Logging Model.* Capture complete request and response payloads for forensic reconstruction and debugging.
* *Metadata-Only, Best-Effort Telemetry Model.* Emit non-blocking metadata sufficient for tracing identity lifecycle events while keeping authoritative state independent. _(Selected)_

== Rationale

Non-blocking telemetry prevents coupling operational infrastructure to identity guarantees.

Metadata-only tracing reduces performance overhead and risk of sensitive data propagation.

Capturing identity lifecycle transitions supports troubleshooting and operational transparency without redefining canonical state semantics.

This model preserves architectural clarity: ERE determines clusters, ERS mirrors outcomes, and telemetry observes behaviour without governing it.

== Consequences

* Telemetry gaps may occur and must be tolerated operationally.
* Observability tooling can evolve independently of clustering and identity contracts.
* Telemetry records may support operational review but do not guarantee completeness or legal evidentiary standards.
* Any introduction of compliance-grade or tamper-proof audit logging would require a new architectural decision.
